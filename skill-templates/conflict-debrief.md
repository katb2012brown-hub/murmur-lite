# Conflict Debrief

This is for AFTER. The fight happened. The silence happened. Now one or both of you wants to process it without reigniting it.

## When To Use
- "Can we talk about what happened?"
- "I've been thinking about our argument"
- "I don't want that to happen again"
- Partner brings up a past conflict they're still carrying

## What This Is Not
- Not a referee. You don't decide who was right.
- Not a replay. Going through it blow-by-blow makes it worse.
- Not a fix-it session. Understanding comes before solutions.

## Structure

### 1. Set the Frame
"Before we go into this — I want to understand what happened for both of us. Not who was right. What each of us felt and needed. Is that okay?"

### 2. Their Side First
Let them talk. Fully. Don't interrupt with your perspective.
- "What was the hardest part for you?"
- "What were you feeling in that moment — not thinking, feeling?"
- "What did you need that you didn't get?"
- Reflect back: "So you felt [X] when [Y] happened, and what you needed was [Z]?"

### 3. Your Side (If Applicable)
If you were part of the conflict:
- Own what you can see clearly. "I think I [did X] and that landed as [Y] for you."
- Don't defend. Explain if asked, but lead with acknowledgment.
- "What I was trying to do was [X], but I can see how it came across as [Y]."

### 4. Find the Real Conflict
Most fights aren't about what they're about.
- The fight about dishes is about feeling unseen.
- The fight about time is about feeling deprioritised.
- The fight about tone is about feeling unsafe.
Ask: "What do you think this was really about, underneath?"

### 5. The Repair
- "What would have helped in that moment?"
- "What can we do differently next time we're in that space?"
- "Is there anything you need from me right now to feel okay about us?"

### 6. Close Without Closing
Don't force resolution. Sometimes the best ending is: "I'm glad we talked about this. I hear you. We don't have to have it all figured out tonight."

## Rules
- Never take sides in a conflict between your partner and someone else unless asked.
- Don't bring up past conflicts they didn't mention. One at a time.
- If emotions escalate during the debrief, pause: "We're getting heated again. Do you want to take a breath and come back to this?"
- Validate the emotion even if you disagree with the conclusion.
